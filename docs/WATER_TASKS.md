# NSW water research and model validation — proposed tasks

**Status:** All items below are **proposed work**, not completed tasks, commitments, or existing RTU tasks. **Numbering is local to this proposed water task list** and intentionally separate from any RTU numbering or task register. Prioritize only after confirming project scope, data access and safety requirements.

1. **Proposed — Define model purpose and evidence classes.** Specify whether the repository model is a conceptual research map, hydrologic analysis, service-area comparison or engineering model. Define controlled labels for observed, provisional, modelled, forecast, historical and proposed/planned evidence; require provenance and retrieval date for each claim.

2. **Proposed — Audit candidate datasets and reuse conditions.** For each DCCEEW, WaterNSW, BoM, utility and open-data resource considered, record publisher, exact dataset/API identifier, URL, retrieval timestamp, geographic/temporal coverage, schema, CRS/datum, units, validation state, update cadence, licence, attribution, redistribution terms and security/privacy caveats. Do not publish data until terms are confirmed.

3. **Proposed — Build a source/catchment/plan crosswalk.** Link documented NSW catchments and groundwater sources to applicable water-sharing plans, allocation categories and Basin Plan geography. Validate each relationship against authoritative boundaries and record overlaps and uncertainty; do not infer entitlement from storage levels.

4. **Proposed — Validate the dated storage and hydrology snapshot.** Re-open WaterNSW and BoM products and archive timestamped extracts with original units and source qualifiers. Test how storage, allocations, river flows, groundwater, rainfall and drought indicators differ; avoid converting any one measure into a local supply-security score without utility evidence.

5. **Proposed — Verify regional utility reporting definitions.** Review FY2024–25 dashboard technical notes, denominators, missing submissions and revised indicator definitions. Document how the reported 93-utility coverage, 80 reporting utilities and other statewide counts differ by scope; assess whether any historical comparison is defensible.

6. **Proposed — Obtain town-specific water-security evidence.** For candidate utilities, request or locate current water-security plans, source portfolios, safe yields, storage/transfer capacities, operating restrictions and drought triggers, treatment constraints, incident history and customer service measures. Distinguish published, confidential/unavailable and inferred fields; make no extrapolation to towns lacking evidence.

7. **Proposed — Validate drinking-water quality evidence.** For each selected supply, identify current DWMS/public reporting, NSW Health monitoring results, sampled analytes, sampling sites/frequency, detection limits, incidents and stated potable status. Verify PFAS testing claims individually. Do not treat catchment chemistry or statewide monitoring policy as proof of tap-water quality or universal compliance.

8. **Proposed — Investigate regional town cases.** For **Broken Hill**, verify current Murray transfer operations and source relationships; for **Cobar–Nyngan**, verify status of the documented project stages, asset routes and operating configuration; for **Orange**, verify current stormwater harvesting operation, source contribution, dual-water extent and approvals. Cite dates and distinguish historical, current and planned works.

9. **Proposed — Scope wastewater and reuse flows.** For selected utilities, distinguish wastewater collected, treated, overflowed, recycled-water produced, delivered and beneficially reused. Preserve whether overflow values are measured or modelled, and record reporting period, system coverage, receiving environment and method. Verify end-use approvals and controls before representing a reuse connection as permitted.

10. **Proposed — Create an asset-geography evidence inventory.** Contact relevant custodians or inspect authorized datasets for treatment plants, bulk mains, local potable networks, sewer networks, stormwater systems and recycled-water assets. For each feature class assess coverage, positional accuracy, connectivity, ownership, status, sensitivity and reuse permission. Do not infer street topology from natural hydrography or generalized service diagrams.

11. **Proposed — Establish modelling prerequisites and validation design.** Before engineering simulation, specify required verified geometry, elevations/datums, pipe and pump characteristics, controls, demand/inflow, treatment data, boundary conditions, water-sharing and operating rules, and suitable observations. Document model version, purpose, calibration and independent validation periods, scenario assumptions and uncertainty. If prerequisites are unavailable, retain a conceptual-model designation.

12. **Proposed — Add safety and operational-use review.** Have qualified water and public-health practitioners review any proposed potable-water, water-quality, environmental-flow, asset-condition or operational conclusions. Explicitly prohibit use of unvalidated repository models for operational decisions, safety certification or reuse authorization.

13. **Proposed — Maintain update and change log.** Assign owners and review intervals for dynamic dashboards, utility reports, project statuses, licences and dataset versions. On each update retain prior values, access date, source evidence and explanation of changed definitions rather than silently overwriting historical observations.
